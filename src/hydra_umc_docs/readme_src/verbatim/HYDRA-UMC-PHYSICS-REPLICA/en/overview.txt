**HYDRA-UMC-PHYSICS-REPLICA** is the kinematics module of the Digital Twin: it reads a URDF kinematic tree (serial arms and branching ones such as a gripper), computes the pose of every joint (forward kinematics) and checks joint positions against their limits. It does not simulate contact or friction and has no physics engine of its own: it computes the joint torques of a motion from the `<inertial>` blocks (inverse dynamics, RNEA), and MuJoCo is only an optional bridge behind a Cargo feature that is off by default.

Units are those of URDF (metres and radians). `fk` prints the position of each joint; with `--json` it prints the position and the rotation as a unit quaternion.

### Key Features:
* 📐 **Kinematic Validation (v0):** real forward kinematics and joint-limit checking over a (documented-partial) URDF subset - see "Honesty check" below for exactly what runs today.
* 🔒 **Real v0 - Limit-Aware FK:** `fk-checked` refuses to compute a world-frame pose for a joint position outside its declared URDF limit, backed by a real, reusable joint-limits corpus and regression tests for both boundaries and wildly out-of-range inputs - closing the gap where plain `fk` would silently report a physically-unreachable pose.
* 🏗️ **URDF to Physics (v0, partial):** reads real `<joint>` elements (type/origin/axis/limit) and walks each joint's real `parent`/`child` link names from the document's own real root link into a chain, in genuine root-to-leaf order regardless of `<joint>` element order in the file. *Not yet real:* collision mesh generation - that's still the "physics" half of this feature.
* ⚡ **Real-Time Performance (planned):** parallelized solving for multi-robot workspaces - depends on a real physics-engine integration existing first.
* 🌡️ **Thermal Simulation (planned):** experimental support for emulating heat dissipation in tool heads (T12/Laser).
* 📋 **What the URDF reader does and does not do (real today):** only `<joint>` elements directly under `<robot>` are read (the joints inside `<transmission>` are ignored), so ros_control files parse; a revolute or prismatic joint needs an `<axis>` of non-zero length and a `<limit>` (without one, `fk-checked` and `validate-limits` refuse to check it); a continuous joint's `<limit>` gives effort and velocity only, and a fixed joint has none; duplicate joint names, a link with two parents and links that no `<link>` declares are errors; `<mimic>`, `<safety_controller>` and floating or planar joints are not supported; a name in `--joints` that the URDF does not have, or one given twice, is an error, and `validate-limits` needs a position for every limited joint unless `--allow-partial` is given (a check that was not made is never reported as "No limit violations").

**Honesty check - what actually runs today:** `fk --urdf PATH --joints "j1=0.5,..."` computes real per-joint world-frame positions by chaining URDF joint transforms, regardless of whether a position is within its declared limit; `fk-checked` runs the same computation but checks every position against `validate-limits`'s real limit check FIRST, refusing to compute (or report) a pose at all if anything is out of range; `validate-limits --urdf PATH --joints "..."` reports real out-of-range joints on its own. All three are pure kinematics - no rigid-body dynamics, no contact forces, no MuJoCo/PhysX solver is wired in yet, and the URDF reader walks the real `parent`/`child` link tree, branches included (every joint is placed from the joint that carries its parent link), works out the joints that follow another one (`<mimic>`) and, when asked, also checks the soft limits of a `<safety_controller>` and the velocity and effort limits (`validate-limits --soft --velocities ... --efforts ...`). See [`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md) for exactly what shipped, and the Roadmap below for what's still ahead.

* 🧮 **Jacobian and inverse kinematics (real, pure kinematics):** `jacobian --urdf PATH --target JOINT --joints "..."` prints the 6 x N geometric Jacobian of the frame of a joint in the world frame (text or `--json`; a joint that follows another through `<mimic>` adds its share to the column of its master), and `ik --urdf PATH --target JOINT --goal X,Y,Z` solves the position by damped least squares with limited steps and positions kept inside the joint limits (`--tolerance`, `--damping`, `--max-step` and `--max-iterations` have defaults). The Jacobian is tested against finite differences of forward kinematics; a goal out of reach is an error, not a pose. A UR5e and a Franka Emika Panda, built from the published Denavit-Hartenberg parameters, check the forward kinematics. See `docs/CLI_REFERENCE.md`.

* 🚧 **Collision prediction by forward kinematics (real, pure kinematics, over-estimates by construction):** `predict-collision --urdf PATH --zones PATH --config PATH --trajectory PATH` says whether a short list of joint states brings the robot into a zone of a HYDRA-UMC-SAFETY-ZONES zones file or into itself. The links are the segments between the joint frames with the radii you declare (there are no defaults), cut into pieces and replaced by spheres that contain them; the joints are interpolated and sampled with a bound on the motion between samples, and a zone is never made smaller. Exit code `0` clear, `1` contact predicted, `2` bad input. Two seeded property tests check that a zone around a point that really lies inside a link is never reported clear. Not tried with a real robot. See `docs/COLLISION_PREDICTION.md`.

* 🌐 **HTTP service (`serve --urdf`, off unless asked for):** `POST /fk`, `/ik`, `/check-trajectory`, `/inverse-dynamics` and `GET|POST /capsules` answer for the URDF it was started with. Loopback only by default, a token is required to listen on another address, the size of a request and the work of one are bounded, a URDF inside a request is refused unless the configuration allows it, and the limits and tolerances live in the configuration file. `/check-trajectory` checks a sampled trajectory against position, speed, acceleration (from the configuration), effort and self-collision before it is sent to the hardware bridge, and says when a check could not run (`complete`). The fixed sentences of the answers exist in the eight languages. Not tried with a real robot. See `docs/SERVICE_API.md`.

* 🧲 **Self-collision with capsules, inverse dynamics and MuJoCo (real, over-estimating where it matters):** `capsules`, `self-collision`, `inverse-dynamics`, `mjcf` and `check-trajectory`. Each sphere, cylinder and box of `<collision>` is replaced by a capsule that contains it (a mesh needs a declared radius); the recursive Newton-Euler algorithm gives the torques from the `<inertial>` blocks and gravity and is checked against closed forms, against the energy balance and against the inverse dynamics of MuJoCo itself; `mjcf` writes the URDF in the model format of MuJoCo. The optional bridge (`--features mujoco`) is off by default. Not tried with a real robot. See `docs/DYNAMICS_AND_COLLISION.md`.

**Shared golden URDF set.** `tests/golden/` holds the same small robots and expected joint-frame positions that HYDRA-UMC-EDITOR-URDF and HYDRA-UMC-EDITOR-STL check, worked out by a plain-math reference, and `tests/golden_urdf.rs` runs `fk` over every case, so the three read `origin`, `rpy`, `axis` and the joint types the same way. The files are copies, kept identical by `tools/golden_sync.py` in HYDRA-UMC-EDITOR-URDF.
