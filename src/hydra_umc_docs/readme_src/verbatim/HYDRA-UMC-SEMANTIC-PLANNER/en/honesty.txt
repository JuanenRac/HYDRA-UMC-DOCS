**Honesty check - what actually runs today:** the closed primitive vocabulary (`primitives.py`), the rule-based task decomposition (`decompose.py`), the rule-based semantic error recovery over a fixed MCU error-code table (`recovery.py`), the precondition validator that refuses a malformed plan rather than passing it on (`validation.py`), and the plain stdlib `http.server` JSON/HTTP surface over the same logic (`api.py`: `POST /decompose`, `POST /recover`, `GET /stats`) are real and tested ({tests} tests, `pytest`), including a fuzz test that runs `decompose_goal()` against hundreds of random/invalid goals with a fixed seed and asserts it never crashes or returns a malformed plan. What is NOT real yet: there is no local LLM anywhere in this codebase - `decompose.py` is regex/template matching over a small, fixed goal vocabulary (assemble/pick-and-place/inspect), not language understanding, and it has never been run against a real Hailo-10 module (this environment doesn't have one). The "Agentic Workflow" and "Hailo-10 Optimized" bullets below are explicitly marked `(planned)` in this same README - they describe the eventual replacement for `decompose_goal()`'s current rule-based kernel, not something that exists today. See `CHANGELOG.md` for exactly what has shipped so far, and the ROADMAP below for what remains open.

**Known limits of the rule-based planner:** a goal that is negated or cancelled ("do not ...", "never ..."), that fits more than one template, that has no object, or that is longer than 512 characters gets no plan. The object is read from the words after the verb, not from the end of the sentence. A decomposition is a proposal, not an order: `POST /decompose` answers `executable: false` with a fingerprint, the estimated cost and a plain-language explanation, and `POST /release` (or `release_for_execution()`) recomputes the cost and the explanation from the plan and checks the approval fingerprint before anything may be executed. The approval is not signed yet: it names a person but is not a credential. `FailureContext.attempt` makes `TIMEOUT` and `GRIP_LOST_SEAL` recoveries escalate to an operator after 1 and 3 automatic attempts.

> **Safety and security status:** this ecosystem is not a certified safety system. There is no hardware E-STOP path, no firmware-enforced limits or interlocks and no verified motion command yet; firmware images are checked with CRC32 and a shared-key HMAC-SHA256, not with an asymmetric signature; TLS and mutual TLS are optional and off by default; no custom board has been populated or tested yet. Read the [security baseline](https://github.com/JuanenRac/HYDRA-UMC-DOCS/blob/main/docs/SECURITY_BASELINE.md) and the [threat model](https://github.com/JuanenRac/HYDRA-UMC-DOCS/blob/main/docs/THREAT_MODEL.md) before relying on any statement here for the safety of a person.
